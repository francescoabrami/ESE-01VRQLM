\subsection{Preparation}


As an initial step of the laboratory activity, the instrumentation was properly configured and verified. 
Following the introductory guidelines provided in the laboratory brief, all equipment was set up according to the prescribed procedure. In particular, the oscilloscope probes were carefully calibrated in accordance with the manufacturer's instructions. The compensation was adjusted to eliminate both under - and over - compensation effects, resulting in an accurately reproduced square-wave signal.

Since ww knew the value of the time base (5 ns/div) and the number of division on which the measure was taken on 12 we computed the sampling frequency and the correlated uncertainty.


The following formula was used:

\begin{equation}
	T_x = \frac{\unit{timebase} \cdot \unit{\#\;division}}{\unit{\#\;dots}}
\end{equation}

The uncertainty on this measure was calculated in this way:

\begin{equation}
	\Delta T_x = T_x \cdot \frac{1}{\unit{\#\;dots}}
\end{equation}

Here a the results obtained after the calculations. We remind that the relative uncertainty of the period $T_x$ is the same for the frequency $f_x$.

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering	

\begin{tabular}{cccc}
\textbf{Object} & \textbf{Measure} & \textbf{Uncertainty $\delta$} & \textbf{Unit}  \\
\hline

$T_{\unit{CH1}}$ & 1.0 & $\pm$ 0.017  & ns \\
\hline
$T_{\unit{CH1,CH2}}$ & 2.0 & $\pm$ 0.067  & ns \\
\hline
$T_{\unit{CH1,CH2,CH3}}$ & 4.0 & $\pm$ 0.267 & ns  \\
\hline
$T_{\unit{CH}}$ & 4.0  & $\pm$ 0.267 & ns  \\

\end{tabular}
\caption*{Sampling period}
\end{table}

We can easily obtain the sampling frequency as reported below.

\begin{equation}
	\Delta f_x = f_x \cdot \frac{\Delta T_x}{T_x} \hspace{0.5cm} \unit{with} \hspace{0.5cm} f_x = \frac{1}{T_x} \hspace{0.5cm} \unit{we\;obtain} \hspace{0.5cm} \Delta f_x = \frac{\Delta T_x}{T^2_x}
\end{equation}

\begin{table}[h!]
\centering	

\begin{tabular}{ccccc}
\textbf{Object} & \textbf{Measure} & \textbf{Uncertainty $\delta$} & \textbf{Uncertainty $\varepsilon_{\%}$} & \textbf{Unit}  \\
\hline

$f_{\unit{CH1}}$ & 1000 & $\pm$ 16.7 & $\pm$ 1.67 \% & Msa/a \\
\hline
$f_{\unit{CH1,CH2}}$ & 500 & $\pm$ 16.7 & $\pm$  3.34 \% & Msa/s \\
\hline
$f_{\unit{CH1,CH2,CH3}}$ & 250 & $\pm$ 16.7 & $\pm$ 6.68 \% & Msa/s   \\
\hline
$f_{\unit{CH}}$ & 250  & $\pm$ 16.7 &  $\pm$  6.68 \%  & Msa/s   \\

\end{tabular}
\caption*{Sampling frequency}
\end{table}


Once we obtained the reported values, we were able to assess their reliability by comparing the calculated sampling frequency with the one displayed in real time by the oscilloscope.

The first and second doubling of the sampling period are due to the logical structure of the digital oscilloscope. Specifically, the instrument we used has a single analog-to-digital converter (ADC), capable of sampling data from a single channel at a maximum frequency of 1 GSa/s (1000 Msa/s), as indicated in the manual. When a second channel is activated, since no additional hardware is available, the instrument performs time-division multiplexing, sampling one channel and then the other alternately, effectively halving the sampling rate, as observed.

When the third channel is activated, this operation is repeated, further halving the already reduced frequency to make room for sampling the fourth channel, even though it is not yet active. Confirming this, it is observed that activating the last channel does not result in any further changes in the data compared to the previous case.
